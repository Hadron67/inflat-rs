The authors study USR inflation in scalar Gauss-Bonnet gravity with lattice
simulations, compare the curvature power spectrum with linear theory, and use it
to compute SIGWs and fit NANOGrav 15-year data. They conclude that lattice
corrections improve the model's fit to the PTA signal. These results are
interesting, but the paper has major problems that need to be addressed before I
can recommend it for publication in PRD.

MAJOR POINTS

1. The Bayesian analysis is not described. The lattice spectra are computed only
at 14 discrete values of $\alpha$ (Eqs.~40--41), yet the sampler explores
$\alpha$ continuously. The manuscript does not explain how the lattice results
enter the likelihood at arbitrary $\alpha$: what is interpolated, how, and how
the spectrum is extended beyond the lattice $k$ range. The prior
$\alpha-\alpha_0 \in [9000, 10200]$ contains only five lattice runs, and the
posterior width ($\pm 100$) is smaller than the grid spacing where the posterior
peaks. As presented, the analysis can be neither reproduced nor assessed. Since
the main conclusion rests on it, this must be clarified, and the robustness of
the posterior to the interpolation should be demonstrated.

2. No lattice convergence tests are presented. The manuscript shows no tests
with respect to resolution, box size, time step, or initial and final simulation
times. These are needed to establish that the lattice spectra, and the reported
deviations from linear theory, are numerically converged. The authors should
present these tests at least for the largest-amplitude cases, which drive their
conclusions.

3. The SIGWs are not computed from the lattice, and this limitation is not
discussed anywhere in the text. The lattice $\mathcal{P}_\zeta$ is inserted into
the standard Gaussian formula (Eq.~52), which discards the non-Gaussianity of
$\zeta$ captured by the $\delta N$ reconstruction. This should be stated clearly
in the abstract and discussed in the text. Lattice computations of SIGWs that
retain this information already exist, and some of them are accompanied with
public codes (e.g.~arXiv:2604.03628, arXiv:2508.10812). They show
$\mathcal{O}(1)$ non-Gaussian corrections to $\Omega_{\rm GW}$ even for mild
non-Gaussianity, comparable to the effects the authors' conclusions rely on. The
authors should state this limitation explicitly, including in the abstract, and
discuss the possible role of non-Gaussianity, which is never mentioned in the
manuscript.

MINOR POINTS

1. Fig.~2 shows $\alpha-\alpha_0 = 0.71\times10^4$, which is not one of the grid
values in Eqs.~(40)--(41). Please clarify.

2. Eq.~(5) seems to be missing a factor. From Eq.~(4), the condition should read
$V' + (V^2/6)\,\xi' = 0$. Unlike Eq.~(5) as written, this is satisfied at
$\phi_c$ with the parameters of Table~I.

3. On p.~4, the authors justify the truncated Wigner approximation by large
occupation numbers. This is correct, but it holds robustly only for
super-horizon modes. At the start of the simulation all modes are sub-horizon,
where the classical treatment relies instead on their evolution being
approximately linear. The authors should comment on this.
